## Method (cited)  

1. **Normalize the input** – Convert the whole string to lower‑case and strip punctuation so that “Word”, “word,” and “word!” are treated identically.  
   *Citation*: Regular‑expression cleaning is the canonical first step for word‑frequency tasks (see the “Word Frequency Counter” article in the evidence [web]).  

2. **Tokenise** – Use `re.findall(r'\b\w+\b', text)` to extract an ordered list of word tokens.  

3. **Count** – Feed the list into `collections.Counter`, which builds a hash‑map of `word → frequency` in O(N) time.  

4. **Select top‑3** – Call `Counter.most_common(3)`. The method returns a list of `(word, count)` tuples already sorted by descending frequency; ties are broken by the order of first appearance in the token list (the behaviour leveraged in the Stack Overflow answer [web:https://stackoverflow.com/questions/63308312/python-get-word-count-frequency-from-a-string]).  

5. **Print** – Iterate over the three tuples and `print(f'({word}, {count})')`. Each tuple appears on its own line, matching the required output format `(word,count)`.  

The entire pipeline is a **pure‑Python** function `print_top3(text: str) → None` that can be imported and invoked from any script or REPL.
